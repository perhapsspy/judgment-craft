# Friction Distillation

1. Separate incidents from proposed remedies. Repetition does not prove a shared mechanism or justify durable structure; different causes, transient friction, and insufficient evidence are valid conclusions.
2. Consider recurrence horizon, cost per recurrence, reversibility, and maintenance burden only when they can change the decision. Choose the lowest sufficient response: correct the current case; state a principle or example; add a rule, template, or checklist; create a reusable skill; automate; change architecture.
3. Test the choice against at least one adjacent lighter or heavier option and state the decisive tradeoff.
4. Add only the ownership and lifecycle controls needed to detect failure, bound exposure, reverse the intervention, or retire it. Require validation before authoritative or externally consequential changes are committed unless exposure is tightly bounded and rollback is reliable. Make explicit the pattern judgment, chosen response or no-structure decision, and only the validation or stop/review condition the risk warrants.
